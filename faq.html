Green Card Process FAQ

Everything you need to understand the green card timeline.

The green card process is often long and country-specific. This FAQ explains the most common steps, terms, and timing questions applicants run into when using the Visa Bulletin and priority date system.

Typical Timeline

Most cases take years, and the exact wait depends on category, country, and whether the filing is in the U.S. or abroad.

Country Matters

India, China, Mexico, and the Philippines often have longer waits because of per-country visa caps and backlog pressure.

Paperwork and Strategy

Strong documentation, early filings, and careful planning can reduce risk, but the Visa Bulletin remains the key gating factor.

Basics

What is a priority date?
A priority date is the date your immigrant petition or labor certification was filed or accepted, depending on the category. It determines where you sit in the queue under the Visa Bulletin. If your priority date is earlier than the bulletin cutoff for your category and country, you may become eligible to file or proceed to the next step.
Basics

What is the Visa Bulletin and why does it matter?
The U.S. Department of State publishes the Visa Bulletin each month. It shows the cutoff dates for each immigrant category and country. The most important thing is not just your petition stage, but whether your priority date is earlier than the current cutoff listed for your category and country. If your date is current, you may be eligible to move to the next step.
Process

What are the main steps in the green card process?
  • Labor certification / PERM for many employment-based cases.
  • I-140 petition filed by the employer or family sponsor.
  • Priority date waiting while the Visa Bulletin backlog clears.
  • I-485 adjustment of status if eligible to file in the U.S., or consular processing abroad.
  • Biometrics, interview, and final decision.
Employment

How do employment-based categories like EB-2 and EB-3 differ?
The category reflects the job and your qualifications. EB-1 is for persons of extraordinary ability or certain priority workers; EB-2 is for advanced degrees or National Interest Waiver cases; EB-3 is for skilled workers, professionals, and other workers. Each category has its own visa quota and backlog. The same country-specific caps can make one category much more delayed than another.
Country

Why do India and China often have the longest waits?
There are annual per-country limits on immigrant visas. When many applicants from a single country are waiting in the same category, the cutoff date moves much more slowly. This is a major reason that India, China, Mexico, and the Philippines may have older dates even when the overall category is moving.
Family

What is the difference between family-based categories?
Family-based categories are grouped by relationship to a U.S. citizen or lawful permanent resident. For example, FB-2A is for spouses and minor children of permanent residents, while FB-4 is for brothers and sisters of adult U.S. citizens. The priority date and relationship often determine how quickly a case can move.
Strategy

What does "current" mean on the Visa Bulletin?
"Current" means there is no backlog in that category/country for the relevant month. In practice, the waiting list is moving and dates may not be strictly current each month. If your priority date is earlier than or equal to the listed cutoff, your case is considered eligible to file or proceed under the current bulletin.
Cross-chargeability

Can a spouse help with a case?
Sometimes yes. If a spouse is born in a country with a faster cutoff, the applicant may be able to use the spouse's country for chargeability under certain rules. This can significantly reduce the wait. It is a technical rule and should be reviewed carefully with counsel or the applicant's immigration records.
Timing

How long does the green card process usually take?
It varies widely. Some categories can move within a year or less once a date becomes current, while others from high-backlog countries may take many years because of visa quotas and category delays. The best way to estimate is to compare your priority date against the latest official cutoff and model the likely movement trend.
Important

Is the calculator or forecast a guarantee?
No. The Visa Bulletin is the official source of record, while forecasts are estimates based on historical movement and policy patterns. Forecasts can be informative but are not promises. Always verify against the latest Department of State bulletin for your category and country.

Need more help?

Immigration law is technical and can change with agency policy, country limits, and filing rules. Use this page as a quick overview, but for case-specific guidance, consult an immigration attorney or official USCIS/State Department sources.
